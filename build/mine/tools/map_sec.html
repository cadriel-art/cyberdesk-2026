How to read this map, and how not to

Two things at once. A pin stands on a place that a source actually names, with a small picture of the type of attack. A numbered box counts the entries where only the country is known, so no pin can be placed; it says nothing about the town, and a line links it to its country when it does not fit inside it.

How sure we are. A filled pin is confirmed: there is solid evidence that it happened. A hollow pin is only reported, claimed or suspected. A grey crossed pin was checked and found not to be an attack, or was never proven. "Not proven" means the evidence is missing, not that nothing happened.

  • It shows what became public, not everything that happened. A high number mostly means that a country reports openly, not that it is the least safe.
  • Where a mark sits: @@NPL@@ entries sit on a named place, @@NAP@@ on an approximate area such as a stretch of sea, and @@NCO@@ are counted in the box on their country, because no source names the town.
  • Month unknown: @@NMO@@ entries carry only a year. They appear under "Up to this month" once the slider passes December of that year, and never under "This month only".
  • Proof level uses the same levels as the evidence tracker: pale is weakest, deep blue is a court verdict. Only cases studied there have a level; every other pin turns pale.
  • Who is named colours a pin only where a court, a prosecutor or a government has formally named a country or group. Claims by the attackers themselves and suspicions by researchers stay pale on purpose.
  • Series that may be linked draws lines from the first entry of a series to the others. A line means "same series", not "same culprit"; the note beside the map says what is and is not proven.
  • One incident, one pin. Where the same incident appears on several pages of this file it is shown once, and later developments such as a verdict sit inside its own card.
